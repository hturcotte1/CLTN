Potentially important issues

1. Lower-dimensional cells can contain stable ﬁxed points (Section 3.1, page 8). The
ﬁnal sentence before Section 3.2 says that lower-dimensional cells are boundary points and are
never SFP. In the cylindrical algebraic decomposition described there, vertical sections above roots
of projection polynomials need not lie on any of the original boundary curves. They can pass
through the open SFP region.
For a concrete example, take G1 from Table 1 and write ∆5 (β, t) for the ﬁfth Hurwitz determinant
of det(µI + K(β, t)). There is exactly one root β0 ∈ (17/8, 15/7) of

Nevertheless, at (β0 , 100) all coordinates of adj(I + β0 N )1 and all six Hurwitz determinants are

These positivity inequalities hold throughout β ∈ [17/8, 15/7] at t = 100. An exact check is to
substitute β = (17/8 + (15/7)u)/(1 + u) into each of the six adjugate coordinates and six Hurwitz
determinants and multiply by (1 + u) to the degree of that polynomial: every coeﬃcient is positive.
The unique root above is obtained by exact root counting in the stated interval.
The issue concerns the claimed pointwise description of the regions. Openness still makes full-
dimensional cells suﬃcient for detecting the existence of an SFP, so this example does not disprove
the census counts or threshold values. The treatment of these sections in the implementation and
recorded regions was not reviewed.


Very minor points

2. Theorem 5.4 does not cover the endpoint used in Lemma 7.4 (page 26). The
proof begins by invoking Theorem 5.4 with ν∞ = 1/2, whereas that theorem explicitly assumes
ν∞ ∈ (1/2, 1). The theorem as stated therefore does not supply the expansion used here. This is a
local justiﬁcation gap; it does not refute Lemma 7.4 or Theorem 7.5.
3. The primitive proportions decrease (Section 6.6, page 23). The paragraph after Table
6 says that the share of primitive counterexamples grows from 3/3 to 102/123 to 6720/9133. These
proportions satisfy 1 > 102/123 = 34/41 > 6720/9133 and hence decrease; the absolute counts
grow.
